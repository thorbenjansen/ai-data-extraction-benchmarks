How to use this supplement

How the 50 benchmarks were selected

Selection prioritizes task fit to A–D, source traceability, distinct diagnostic coverage and relevance to research documents. Scores, model rankings and apparent improvement were not selection criteria. Closely related variants remain within their existing family.

The full 82-family atlas is preserved separately as the source archive. The 32 exclusions include remote mathematics/engineering tasks, conversation/puzzle tasks, screening-only work, and benchmarks primarily about instruction compliance, sycophancy or factuality outside A–D. This selection is a reasoned editorial choice, not an objective or exhaustive ranking of benchmark quality.

Each included family has a task-specific rationale, at least one A–D link and at least one extraction-workflow link. Existing mappings are retained; Manalyzer additionally maps to C because its Table 4 explicitly evaluates calculation-dependent extraction. Tables and figures (legacy A2) map to A.

4,094 source observations; 1,716 plotted observations from 40 families; 391 separate conditions. Scores, model dates and descriptive curves are unchanged.

Curve estimates remain descriptive within each original condition. This selection does not establish overall model progress, comparative human accuracy, or successful transfer to psychology.

Coverage and limits

The article selection contains 50 benchmark families and 54 benchmark-version entries from the original 82-family archive. It is a curated evidence map, not an exhaustive systematic review. Versions, subtasks and shared component datasets are not independent studies.

The workflow map links each benchmark to a concrete operation or requirement. “Direct extraction task” means direct within the benchmark's stated domain; it does not mean validated transfer to psychological evidence synthesis. “Component task” measures a relevant subskill. “Indirect proxy” has more distant task or population fit. A mapping is not evidence that a workflow recommendation improves accuracy.

Scores and model release dates

The start page follows the extraction workflow vertically and presents benchmark figures horizontally at each step. The navigation order is an operational reading sequence; coordination, collaboration and documentation can span several stages. Raw observations are opened by clicking a figure.

Each time plot uses the full eligible release-date range of its own source/metric/test condition. No eligible older observation is excluded by a fixed date window. The axis includes the earliest and latest release-month interval where available, plus a margin of 6% of the span (at least 14 and at most 60 days; 30 days for a single exact date), capped at the 31 October 2026 evidence cutoff. Conditions with no eligible date show a gap without invented calendar ticks. Calendar ranges differ and the apparent visual slope cannot be compared without accounting for the time scale. Common-calendar SVG variants retain a shared global range for comparison. Fitted means keep their full documented input and supported temporal segments. A dated checkpoint, benchmark Date column or result/submission date is not automatically a public model release. Only documented release dates, source-reported release months or exact identity matches to such documented releases receive time positions. Full-month intervals are shown for month-only dates. Where no release date is established, the graph shows the gap and the source data remain available. See release_date_audit.csv for every inclusion and omission.

All 4,940 values retain the original metric, unit, task condition and source locator. There is no pooled accuracy score. Known differences in splits, context lengths, inputs, prompts and tools remain separate. Model release dates do not identify when an evaluation ran or isolate why performance differed. Generic rolling aliases are not assigned a release snapshot unless the result source already identifies the release cohort.

Twelve observations are approximate readings from published figures: four inherited MRCR values and eight common-ground trial rates. They carry ≈. They are excluded from the strict dated comparisons in the prior figure collection. No uncertainty was invented. Source intervals, standard errors and undefined ± terms are not treated as equivalent confidence intervals.

Descriptive mean curves

The dashed gold curve estimates the mean of reported model configurations within one source, metric, benchmark version and task condition. It does not pool native scores across benchmarks. All individual points remain visible. Repeated results for the same named configuration and release are averaged first, giving each configuration one weight. Human references, approximate figure readings and undated results do not enter the fit.

We use a Gaussian-kernel regression with a fixed bandwidth of 180 days. A curve requires at least five dated configurations, four distinct release-date labels and a span of 90 days. It is drawn only between the first and last observed release dates, with local effective sample size of at least two configurations and an observation within one bandwidth. Longer unsupported gaps remain open. Where these criteria are not met, the figure states why no curve is fitted.

This is a descriptive mean of the selected published configurations. Changing model composition, model size, training, prompting, contamination, evaluation timing and other differences can explain changes over time. The curve estimates neither a causal effect of calendar time nor the performance of a representative population of all available models. No confidence band is invented from this heterogeneous convenience sample.

Calculation and sensitivity

At date t, the mean is the sum of w_i(t) y_i divided by the sum of w_i(t), with w_i(t) = exp(−0.5 ((t_i − t) / 180 days)²). Month-only releases use the same interval midpoint as their plotted mark; the full month remains visible. Identical date/score marks may overlap, but their original inputs remain separate unless they repeat the same configuration.

Alternative bandwidths of 90 and 365 days are retained as sensitivity calculations. They are not confidence intervals. The complete inputs, evaluation grid and fitting decisions are downloadable: inputs with observation IDs, all three smoothing grids, decisions for every condition, method specification.

Current coverage: 94 source/task conditions in 25 benchmark families support a curve. A missing curve is not evidence that performance has remained unchanged.

Recomputed released predictions

Eighteen BABILong rates were recomputed using the published scoring rule and released predictions, with no new model calls. Sixteen files use matching 100-case question/target sets within each task. References and source snapshots

Each point has a result reference, exact URL, cell/page locator, access/audit date and source hash. The benchmark citation and result citation are separate. Primary bibliographic metadata or the official repository's recommended citation supplies author, year and title. ArXiv references identify the accessed preprint; a published version is used where the primary metadata or official citation establishes it. Undated repositories, datasets and leaderboards retain n.d. and a retrieval date. An account name is retained where a personal or corporate author was not established.

The 44 inherited Figure 1 observations retain their prior audit. Their SHA-256 identifies the local audited input CSV, not the remote webpage. Two inherited date pages (Pixtral-12B and the Gemini-2.5 family) were unavailable on refresh; the earlier audit remains the basis. The full source snapshots are preserved in the preceding figure package; this supplement bundles the values, bibliographic metadata and provenance rather than redistributing every original PDF.

Human references and workflow evaluation

Agreement with a human extraction is not an independent measurement of its accuracy. Benchmark reference labels were not re-adjudicated for this map. Human observations remain specific to the source task, instructions and population. This evidence establishes neither a general human advantage nor a general LLM advantage. The target comparison is source-based accuracy at each step, plus corrections, introduced errors, omissions, time and the consequences of combining contributions.

Access and reproducibility

The supplement is a portable, static HTML collection. Navigation, figures, filtering and point-to-reference links work without an external library or model service. External source links require internet access. Each benchmark's time-axis PDF and SVG can be downloaded; every value is also available in CSV. The artifact contains no credentials and performs no new model evaluations.

Manuscript anchors refer to the revised clean manuscript's zero-based OOXML paragraph order (the title is paragraph 0); section descriptions identify the relevant passage. The workflow table operationalizes those requirements with explicit manuscript anchors. The separate commentary inventory links the 29 claims in the English Claims workbook to relevant benchmark tasks and their interpretive boundaries.
