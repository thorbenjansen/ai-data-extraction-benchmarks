Methods for the benchmark supplement

Eight benchmark families, 27 separate test conditions and 650 dated observations provide illustrative time trends for skills relevant to data extraction.

Interactive data points

Hover over or focus a point to see its model name, original score, metric, test condition, release evidence and APA result reference. Click, tap or press Enter to keep the details open. The source-row link opens the exact observation and its full provenance. Overlapping observations can be selected individually within the panel. Escape or the close button dismisses the panel.

The published values and point positions are source-bound. Adding interactivity does not alter the scores, dates, conditions or fitted curves. Point metadata · Displayed dated observations.

Current selection: well-populated positive time trends

The main page was reduced on 4 November 2026 to eight benchmark families, 650 dated source observations and 27 separate test conditions. All four commentary challenges remain represented. This is an outcome-selected set of illustrative positive trajectories, not an estimate of the frequency or average magnitude of progress across benchmarks.

Minimum data within a comparable condition

A condition must contain at least 10 model configurations with exact source scores, six distinct documented release positions and 180 days of coverage, with an existing supported mean curve. Benchmark version, metric, split, prompting, tools and other recorded test conditions remain separate. Repeated results for a model configuration and release are averaged first; benchmark question counts are not model sample sizes. Release-month intervals retain their original midpoint positioning.

Positive trend and robustness

At each release position, we take the median native score across the available configurations, so multiple models released on the same date do not count as separate temporal positions. A qualifying condition requires (1) a positive direction-adjusted Theil–Sen median pairwise slope, (2) a higher median for the latest third than for the earliest third of release positions, with at least two positions per group, (3) a date-level Kendall tau-a of at least .30, and (4) a positive first-to-last change over the supported portions of each existing Gaussian-kernel curve at bandwidths of 90, 180 and 365 days. Lower-is-better metrics reverse direction for these checks. Ties contribute zero to tau-a; no significance threshold or invented confidence interval is used. Slopes retain their original units and are not pooled across benchmarks.

A family is retained if at least one condition qualifies and no other adequately sampled condition has a nonpositive slope, early–late contrast or supported-curve change. The main page shows every adequately sampled condition in each retained family, including weaker positive associations. This avoids choosing only favorable subtasks within an otherwise mixed family. BFCL is archived because its overall score improves while adequately sampled subtasks regress. DocMath-Eval has ample data but weaker date-level associations. Other exclusions largely reflect sparse comparable dated observations, rather than demonstrated absence of progress.

Interpretation and preserved evidence

These thresholds are transparent editorial choices for selecting illustrative time trends. They are not preregistered or established standards. Model composition, task difficulty and observed periods differ. The figures cannot estimate general progress, establish a causal effect of release date, or validate transfer to psychological data extraction. All candidate screening decisions are documented.

How the figures are ordered from left to right

Within each challenge, the existing default diagrams are ordered by the first-to-last change across the supported parts of their displayed 180-day mean curve, from larger to smaller gains. We divide by the metric’s native axis maximum to express the gain as a fraction of the plotted score axis. The current eight default figures all use a 0–100 axis. Equal gains are ordered by date-median tau-a and then configuration count; figure ID is the final tie-breaker. The existing default condition is retained for every family; no more favorable subtask is substituted to determine its position.

This is an illustrative display order. Test tasks, difficulty and observed time spans differ, so the ordering is neither a common performance effect size nor a ranking of benchmark quality or extraction validity. All other conditions remain available through the selectors. Exact order, source condition IDs and calculation values.

Model release dates and comparable conditions

X positions use documented public model releases or source-reported release months. Model aliases must identify the evaluated release cohort; a benchmark publication, checkpoint filename or result date is not substituted for a release date. Month-only dates retain their full interval and use its midpoint for positioning. Missing or unresolved dates are not invented.

Each figure spans the documented eligible release history of its own source, metric, benchmark version and test condition. Calendar ranges can differ between figures. Prompts, tools, reasoning modes, splits, context lengths and budgets remain separate whenever recorded as different evaluation conditions. Scores retain their native units; no pooled cross-benchmark accuracy is calculated.

Descriptive mean curves

The dashed gold curve estimates the mean of reported model configurations within one source, metric, benchmark version and task condition. It does not pool native scores across benchmarks. All individual points remain visible. Repeated results for the same named configuration and release are averaged first, giving each configuration one weight. Human references, approximate figure readings and undated results do not enter the fit.

We use a Gaussian-kernel regression with a fixed bandwidth of 180 days. A curve requires at least five dated configurations, four distinct release-date labels and a span of 90 days. It is drawn only between the first and last observed release dates, with local effective sample size of at least two configurations and an observation within one bandwidth. Longer unsupported gaps remain open. Where these criteria are not met, the figure states why no curve is fitted.

This is a descriptive mean of the selected published configurations. Changing model composition, model size, training, prompting, contamination, evaluation timing and other differences can explain changes over time. The curve estimates neither a causal effect of calendar time nor the performance of a representative population of all available models. No confidence band is invented from this heterogeneous convenience sample.

Calculation and sensitivity

At date t, the mean is the sum of w_i(t) y_i divided by the sum of w_i(t), with w_i(t) = exp(−0.5 ((t_i − t) / 180 days)²). Month-only releases use the same interval midpoint as their plotted mark; the full month remains visible. Identical date/score marks may overlap, but their original inputs remain separate unless they repeat the same configuration.

Alternative bandwidths of 90 and 365 days are retained as sensitivity calculations. They are not confidence intervals. The complete inputs, evaluation grid and fitting decisions are downloadable: inputs with observation IDs, all three smoothing grids, decisions for every condition, method specification.

Scope of the evidence

The figures illustrate component capabilities. No displayed benchmark is a dedicated test of codebook-based extraction from psychological studies or a validation of a complete human–AI workflow. The workflow links and A–D assignments indicate task relevance. Shared datasets, subtasks and benchmark versions are not independent studies.

Agreement with human-extracted review data does not by itself establish either actor’s accuracy. Human-only, LLM and joint workflows require independent source verification and comparable product-accuracy and process-auditability criteria.

The screening record covers 50 candidate families and documents why a family is included in or excluded from the displayed progress examples. Mixed findings and insufficient dated observations remain documented. Screening record · Family decisions · Condition decisions.

Sources and reproducibility

Each score retains its primary result reference, URL, locator, access date, metric and condition. Model release sources are recorded separately. Where source access or model identity remains uncertain, the recorded status is retained. Benchmark citations describe the evaluation task; result citations identify the evidence for a numerical value.

The evidence cutoff is 31 October 2026. Curves summarize reported model configurations; no new model evaluation was conducted. Reproducibility materials document the data, date decisions, calculation inputs, chart geometry and descriptive smoothing method.
